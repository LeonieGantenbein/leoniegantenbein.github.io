CLEAR Labelling AI Involvement in Texts

Conception · Language · Evidence · Attribution · Responsibility

Leonie Gantenbein · Version 1.0 · 2026 · Licence CC BY 4.0

Overview

CLEAR is a proposal for disclosing the involvement of artificial intelligence in texts voluntarily and in a differentiated way. The blanket label “AI-generated” is replaced by five statements. They record in which stages of the work an AI took part, how far its contribution extends and who stands behind the result. Each person involved makes these statements for their own share. Like a citation, CLEAR is therefore not a judgement but an account of how a text came into being.

Culture first, then labelling. A label can only take effect where disclosure is not penalised. As long as an honest statement devalues one’s own text, only those who can afford it will label, and silence remains the rational choice. CLEAR therefore presupposes a cultural understanding that has yet to be reached. Chapter “Culture before Labelling”

The highest level reached is given for each axis; it includes the levels below. Each person involved – such as author, editor or translator – gets a line of their own. The label appears before or after the text.

Example

CLEAR (author): C1, L3, E(p)1, A: Claude Opus 4.8, Fable 5, Opus 5, 04–09/2026, R1

The chapter “Specification” contains the full description of all levels, guidance on classification and the application in educational institutions.

All abbreviations at a glance, together with a prompt that lets a language model declare its own involvement, can be found in the factsheet at the end of this page.

Status

CLEAR is a proposal that has not yet been tested or evaluated in practice, and it is certainly incomplete. Feedback, experience from use and proposals for images, moving images and code are expressly welcome – via the issues in the GitHub repository.

Origin

CLEAR was developed in the book Zwischen Prompt und Welt. Über das Leben mit KI [Between Prompt and World: On Living with AI] by Leonie Gantenbein, forthcoming in December 2026 from Schwabe Verlag. It builds on concepts from Annetzung. Handeln durch Technologie und Imagination [Acting through Technology and Imagination] (2025), in particular the concept of world transparency (Welttransparenz): the claim to know whether one is facing something actual, something computer-generated or something imagined.

Factsheet

The factsheet summarises all abbreviations and rules of CLEAR. It serves as a quick reference and can be given to a language model as a prompt so that the model declares its involvement in a text according to CLEAR.

Notation

CLEAR (role): C?, L?, E(p|n)?, A: models, period, R?

The highest level reached is given for each axis; it includes the levels below. Each person involved gets a line of their own. The label appears before or after the text.

AbbreviationMeaning
C – Conception: conception and line of thought
C0The line of thought was developed exclusively by humans.
C1The AI served as a discussion partner during conception.
C2Individual content parts were worked out by the AI.
C3The content was comprehensively generated by the AI.
L – Language: linguistic editing
L0The language was formulated without AI.
L1Proofreading: spelling, grammar and punctuation.
L2Stylistic editing: wording improved, content unchanged.
L3Substantive editing: interventions in structure and argument.
L4Translation by the AI.
E – Evidence: sources and how they were processed
E0Source access and processing without AI.
E1Source access: the AI finds, obtains or translates sources; the human reads and interprets them.
E2Source processing: the AI summarises sources; work continues with the summaries.
E3Source interpretation: the AI’s interpretation of the sources was adopted.
E4Interpretation of results: a conclusion drawn by the AI appears in the text as a result.
(p)Published material that readers can verify.
(n)Own, unpublished material.
A – Attribution: declaration of the AI models used and the period of their use
R – Responsibility: accountability
R1Fully checked and taken responsibility for by the human.
R2Spot-checked and therefore not fully taken responsibility for.
R3Published unchecked and no one’s responsibility.

Prompt for a language model’s self-declaration

The following text can be copied into the same chat at the end of a collaboration. The model then proposes a label and gives reasons for it.

Please label, according to CLEAR, how you were involved in this text. Base your answer exclusively on our conversation so far.

For each axis, give the highest level reached and justify it in one sentence:

C – Conception: C0 line of thought exclusively human · C1 AI as a discussion partner · C2 individual content parts worked out by the AI · C3 content comprehensively generated by the AI
L – Language: L0 without AI · L1 proofreading · L2 stylistic editing (wording, content unchanged) · L3 substantive editing (structure and argument) · L4 translation
E – Evidence: E0 without AI · E1 source access (finding, obtaining, translating) · E2 source processing (summaries replace the primary source) · E3 source interpretation adopted · E4 the AI’s conclusion adopted as a result; suffix (p) published, (n) own unpublished material
A – Attribution: your model name and the period of our collaboration
R – Responsibility: Leave R open. Only the human can make this statement.

Format: CLEAR (role): C?, L?, E(p|n)?, A: model, period, R?
For L, the deepest involvement counts, not the most frequent. If you cannot determine a level with certainty from the conversation, say so instead of guessing.

Limits of the self-declaration

A model’s self-declaration is only meaningful if the entire work took place with a single language model in a single conversation. Only then does the model have an overview of the complete working process. If several chats, tools or models were used, if the text was revised outside the chat, or if the conversation is so long that earlier parts are no longer taken into account, the model cannot fully assess its involvement. The classification must then be made by the person themselves.

Even in the most favourable case, the model’s statement remains a proposal that the human checks and corrects where necessary. Only the human can take on responsibility (R).
